Who are we?

What is Adabida?

Adabida seeks to document the point of contact and exchange between personal biography and the history of societies, understanding that each life is, at the same time, an individual experience and a historical testimony. In this way, Adabida is a digital historical archive dedicated to preserving oral memory and the personal legacy of individuals. When personal biography comes into contact with collective history, the archive is born.

It offers a safe and respectful space where anyone can document their life through written testimonies, audio, video and photographs. It is not a social network: it is an archive. A place designed to preserve, not to consume. To remember, not to forget.

Adabida is conceived as a project with academic vocation and historiographical rigor. The content of the archive is generated by users themselves through three main channels: individual profiles, family spaces and organizations (institutions, cultural associations, memorial entities, clubs, etc.). The goal is to create a documentary oral memory fund accessible to researchers, but also to anyone who wishes to preserve their legacy or that of their family.

The Adabida team

The Adabida team is made up of historians, psychologists and journalists committed to preserving oral memory and personal heritage.

We have observed a worrying paradox: we live in the era with the greatest technical capacity to preserve individual memory, yet we are at risk of losing unique primary sources from one of the most fascinating periods in human history. We have more elderly people than ever before in our society, yet we listen to them less than ever. Until a few generations ago, elders were listened to and revered as sources of knowledge and wisdom. Today, older people — custodians of irrepeatable experiences of the 20th century — are often ignored or undervalued. Their testimonies about wars, social transformations and unprecedented technological changes risk disappearing with them every day.

Adabida was born to reverse this paradox: to put technology at the service of those who have the most to tell and are given the least voice. It was born to preserve the place where individual life becomes collective history.

Ethical manifesto

Adabida is governed by fundamental ethical principles:

1. Free technologies and corporate independence

Adabida does not depend on large technology corporations. It uses free software and independent servers. The code is transparent and auditable. This guarantees technological sovereignty and real control over data.

2. Your data is yours

Adabida will never sell, share or exploit user data. Everyone has the right to digital erasure and data portability. Adabida guarantees this right.

3. We are not a product

There is no advertising. There is no tracking. There are no algorithms designed to manipulate. Adabida exists to serve people, not to exploit them.

4. Rigor and transparency with memory

Adabida clearly distinguishes between personal memory and verified historical fact. Testimonies are preserved as people recount them, but always with transparency regarding their nature: they are primary sources, not absolute truths. This allows their academic use with rigor.

5. Universal accessibility

Adabida is and will always be 100% free. In addition, it is designed to be easy to use for everyone, regardless of age or digital experience. No one should be excluded for economic or technical reasons.

6. Respect and dignity

Each story is treated with the respect and dignity it deserves. Memories are not commodities.

Adabida Project

Adabida Project serves two purposes: one academic and one social. On the one hand, it preserves historical testimonies of academic value as direct primary sources accessible to anyone on the planet. On the other, it dignifies legacy and gives meaning to the lives of many people who, despite being part of history, are often not listened to, despite the potential importance of their memories and experiences.

Transparent funding

Adabida is a 100% free service for users, but its development and maintenance have real costs. The project is funded exclusively through voluntary contributions from the community.

Adabida operates under a different model: use is free, but costs (servers, development, maintenance) are assumed by the community in a transparent and voluntary manner. No one is required to pay, but those who value the project can contribute to making it sustainable.

Why this model?

  • Independence: Adabida does not depend on corporations or investors
  • Privacy: User data is not the product
  • Ethics: Memories must not be monetized
  • Community: The project belongs to everyone and is for everyone

Important notice

⚠️ The testimonies stored in Adabida are authentic personal memories, but they are NOT verified by professional historians.

What does this mean?

Adabida preserves memory as people remember and share it. This means:

  • Testimonies reflect personal perceptions and memories
  • There may be errors in dates, names or events
  • Human memory is subjective and may change over time
  • Adabida does not verify facts or cross-check sources

Freedom of expression and neutrality

Adabida defends freedom of expression and offers an open space where anyone can preserve their memories and testimonies, regardless of political position, religious beliefs or origin.

Adabida is not a debate forum nor a space for historical interpretation. It is an archive of primary sources: the individual voices that make up collective memory.

User verification system

Adabida includes an identity verification system based on documentation provided by users themselves (ID cards, birth certificates, etc.). Profiles with verified documentation are marked with a visual indicator (green), but this only indicates that documents have been submitted. Under no circumstances can Adabida validate the authenticity of this documentation. This system is purely indicative within the project and has no scientific or legal validity.

Academic responsibility

If you are a researcher, historian or journalist, you must:

  • Cross-check information with other primary and secondary sources
  • Apply critical methodology
  • Not treat testimonies as valid without independent verification
  • Properly cite the source and context

The value of testimonies

Even so, personal testimonies have immense historical and human value. They offer:

  • Personal perspectives on historical events
  • Emotional and human context
  • Details of everyday life
  • Voices often excluded from official history

Adabida is an oral memory archive, not a verified historical archive. It values memory as human heritage, always maintaining the necessary critical perspective.
